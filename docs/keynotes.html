Reality Is the Final Verifier: On Two Key Gaps in Agentic Software Engineering

Ion Stoica / EECS Department at the University of California at Berkeley

Software development iterates between implementation and verification until an evaluator accepts the result. Yet even a formal proof establishes correctness only relative to stated requirements and a modeled environment, not stakeholder intent or the real world. We introduce the two-gap framework to characterize these mismatches: the requirement gap and the model gap. Reward hacking exploits omissions in either; hallucination widens them by fabricating requirements or assumptions. Because open, changing systems cannot generally close these gaps, we propose an assurance-revision loop that uses deployment evidence to revise requirements, models, and evaluators. We frame assured agentic development as allocating scarce human judgment, agent capability, and compute: judgment narrows the requirement gap, while faithful evaluation narrows the model gap. Reality remains the final verifier.
